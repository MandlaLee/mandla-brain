# 15 — Photography, Illustration, Icons and Art Direction

## Images must belong to the world of the brand
Visual assets are not decorative filler. Photography, illustration, icon geometry, motion graphics, textures and data visualization should express a consistent artistic language and support comprehension. When a design uses unrelated stock pictures and icon sets, it feels generated and interchangeable even if the colour palette is attractive.

## Art direction begins with purpose
Define what each image must communicate:
- identify a real person, place, product, process or achievement;
- show evidence of quality, community, scale, use or outcome;
- establish an emotional narrative or atmosphere;
- create repeatable recognition assets;
- aid navigation/instruction;
- supply illustration where photography cannot represent an abstract process.

Choose the medium according to communicative purpose. A photo of a real township restaurant may be credible where generic AI-generated food imagery is misleading. A simple original line diagram may communicate a software workflow better than a stylized 3D render.

## Photographic grammar
Establish rules for:
1. **Subjects:** what/who appears and why, with permission and accuracy.
2. **Setting:** on-location versus studio; environment as identity.
3. **Camera perspective:** eye level, low-angle, close-up, wide context.
4. **Lighting:** natural, hard, soft, high/low key, time of day.
5. **Composition:** negative space, alignment, action direction, contextual props.
6. **Colour grade:** warmth/coolness, contrast, saturation, natural skin tones.
7. **Crop ratios:** hero, carousel, thumbnail, poster, avatar, story and horizontal banner.
8. **Retouching:** allowed dust cleanup/colour correction versus misleading alterations.
9. **Caption/credit:** photographer and subject credits, alt descriptions, consent.
10. **Format:** exact export dimensions, compression, colour profile and naming.

Do not flatten different skin tones into one generic “brand grade.” Lighting must preserve natural appearance while matching environmental context. For composites, direction of shadows, perspective and colour temperature must agree.

## Image originality and rights
Rights differ between original commissioned assets, licensed stock, permissively licensed illustrations, generated images, user-owned photos and copyrighted editorial photographs. Document: copyright holder, licence, allowed channels, modification rights, model/venue releases, attribution, expiry and source files. Publicly viewable does not mean freely reusable. A screenshot of another product may be used in private research documentation where permitted, but should not become a marketing asset without rights review.

## Icon system
Icons communicate repeated concepts. Choose one grammar for stroke width, optical size, corner treatment, cap/join, filled-versus-outline, metaphoric convention and states. Avoid random mixing of three incompatible libraries. Specify how icons combine with accessible names: unlabeled icon-only controls may be acceptable with visible tooltip and keyboard/screen-reader labels, but test discoverability.

### Semantic icon rules
Icons must support labels; they should not be required to decipher arbitrary metaphors. Distinguish destructive action, alert, info, upload, share, saved, live/offline and navigation using familiar shapes where possible. A custom brand icon can be original, but avoid making essential actions novel puzzles. Check at 16, 20, 24, 32 and 48 px as relevant.

## Illustration systems
Define a visual vocabulary: line weight, perspective (flat/isometric/oblique), shape construction, stroke joins, palette ratios, shading, textures, detail density, human anatomy/face accuracy, environment and narrative tone. Produce a reusable specimen sheet with 3–5 distinct subjects and verify that they feel from the same world.

### Game art
For a sprite-based game:
- define camera angle, grid/pixels-per-unit, anchor, scale and frame alignment;
- create sprite inventory with transparent masters, resolution and frame counts;
- test assets assembled in actual gameplay before producing dozens of variants;
- preserve character proportions between animations, including shadows and directional facing;
- distinguish decorative background from collision geometry and interactable entities;
- keep textual UI accessible and sufficiently contrasted against textured scenes.
An attractive scene render does **not** replace separate usable sprites. Label source versus assembled proof, and test in-game rather than only in a Photoshop-like mockup.

## Album art and creator identity
Album covers can be expressive and unconventional, but require coherent photography/lettering/colour across digital thumbnails, vertical music visuals and press. Identity should not erase face/artist consistency or give a song a false commercial affiliation. Establish a series rule where the cover art, title, motion teaser, social captions and banners share visual DNA.

## Typography and imagery relationship
Plan focal area and text placement together. A type overlay with insufficient contrast, covering a face or principal subject, is a composition defect. Use alternate crops for different aspect ratios rather than expecting one original image to fit all destinations. Test small thumbnails; poster titles may remain legible while subtext does not.

## Generative imagery and source ethics
Generated imagery can help concept exploration and original stylization, but shouldn't be passed off as a literal documentary image of a real customer or venue. For user-supplied faces, preserve recognizable features according to user instructions and be transparent about edits. If a project requires exact existing logos/assets, use original files rather than approximate regeneration. Record output provenance and relevant use restrictions.

## Production pipeline
1. Art direction brief with references and exclusions.
2. Rights, releases and authenticity check.
3. Source capture or original generation.
4. Edit toward approved palette and camera/illustration grammar.
5. Export responsive variants and transparent cutouts where needed.
6. Attach alt text and captions.
7. Test in real layout / game engine / social thumbnail.
8. Run full asset-size, clipping, missing-file and mobile performance checks.
9. Archive editable masters and rights register.

## Red flags
Faces mismatched across campaigns; overprocessed skin; generic office photos unrelated to local services; icon mixed styles; inconsistent shadows; illegible text over background; wrong camera angles for sprites; generated “real venue” imagery where accuracy matters; licensed assets committed to public GitHub; social logo copy-paste.
