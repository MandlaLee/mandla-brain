# 00 — Design Director Charter

## The standing design requirement
For **every** branding, identity, logo, UI, website, app, poster, game interface, album design, icon, packaging or visual polish request, read the [library index](README.md), this charter and the relevant specialist chapters **before choosing or implementing a design direction**. This is a durable Mandla Brain rule. Never claim the repository was consulted unless actually accessed.

## Definition of quality
Design is successful when it is **appropriate, recognizable, useful, accessible, distinctive, technically reliable, production-ready and coherent through time**. It must represent the project accurately. A good-looking screenshot is not enough. A functioning app with a default AI dashboard skin is not done. A beautiful logo that fails a one-colour print or 16-pixel reduction is not done.

### Seven design obligations
1. **Truth:** Show current and accurate product state; never invent client claims, a live feed, social proof or verified outcomes.
2. **Context:** Build for the real audience, device, environment, content and use case—not a generic template.
3. **Originality:** Study public products, then synthesize a distinctive original system. Do not clone proprietary logos, artwork or layouts wholesale.
4. **Function:** Primary tasks, navigation, keyboard access, feedback, forms and error handling must work.
5. **System:** Use shared type, grid, colour roles, tokens, components, icon style, tone and interaction patterns. Avoid a collection of unrelated screens.
6. **Craft:** Inspect actual rendered output, align optical details, test at native sizes and revise defects.
7. **Preservation:** Commit files, preserve original vectors/source, describe decisions and provide an inspectable artifact.

## Decision hierarchy
Current explicit instruction > legal/safety/accessibility requirement > current canonical project identity > verified project evidence > this design handbook > outside reference patterns > fashionable visual trends. A cross-project guide must never silently rewrite approved brand names or project roles.

## Generic AI design failure patterns
- The same rounded rectangle repeated over every panel, regardless of content or task.
- Dark navy dashboard plus one arbitrary neon accent for every software product.
- Fake metrics, generic glass effects, decorative gradients, irrelevant geometric overlays.
- “Innovating the future” copy without a factual service or product proposition.
- Three equally weighted cards where the user needs one large, continuously visible tool.
- Over-spaced “luxury” layouts that reduce working information density.
- Stock portraits with demographic tokens rather than real, consented photography.
- Random illustration styles, inconsistent shadows and line widths, poor icon alignment.
- Repeated hero/services/testimonial structure where the user needs a different information architecture.
- A logo that just combines initials and a generic category symbol without ownership or originality testing.
These are not universally forbidden forms; they fail when used without a task- and brand-specific rationale.

## The logo-off test
Hide the mark and name. Do typography, distinctive shapes, spacing rhythm, copy style, photographic treatment, layout and behaviour still feel like one brand rather than a themed template? If not, develop those distinctive assets. This is a heuristic, not a validated recall study; use actual attribution testing when available.

## Design direction before surface styling
For substantial work, compare at least three fundamentally different routes. Example for a technical product:
- **Instrument:** dense precision, legible data, compact chrome, strong hierarchy.
- **Editorial:** stronger typography, deliberate negative space, high-contrast information sequence.
- **Expressive:** signature shape, motion and culturally/contextually grounded language.
Routes must differ in architecture and information arrangement, not simply swap one accent colour.

## Eight quality gates
**A. Brief** — audience, product purpose, content, risk, budget, constraints and current canon captured.
**B. Evidence** — current working files inspected; relevant references reviewed with strengths and limitations.
**C. Concept** — distinctive thesis and explicit reasons for type, grid, colour, motif and primary task emphasis.
**D. Prototype** — real content, responsive variants and major error/empty/loading states.
**E. Implementation** — component semantics, design tokens, correct behaviour and platform conventions.
**F. Visual review** — screenshot or print proof inspected at intended size; tiny spacing and overflow corrected.
**G. Experience review** — keyboard, screen reader where applicable, contrast, reduced motion, slow/offline and task completion checked.
**H. Handover** — final files committed, links verified, licensing checked, open issues recorded.

An urgent small change can use a compact version of these gates, but executable production design requires actual inspection.

## Evidence labels
**Normative**: published standard or platform requirement. **Supported**: systematic review, validated experiments or repeatable user research. **Observed**: pattern in shipped products and codebases, not guaranteed transferable. **Heuristic**: reasoned craft recommendation. **Hypothesis**: testable proposition needing research. Marketing “psychology facts” without quality evidence are neither normative nor supported.

## High-stakes financial/trading design
Mark BUY and SELL as signals, not executions. Show chart timeframe, source timeframe, confirmation time, reference price, SL, TP, data timestamp, stale/offline/demo status and confidence limitations. Do not infer a broker fill from the price used by the model. Colour is redundant to textual and spatial information. Avoid fabricated certainty. Backtests require honest execution and look-ahead assumptions. Use desktop screen space intentionally; allow collapsible tools and keyboard commands.

## Cultural responsibility
Do not use Xitsonga words, South African symbols, historic marks or township imagery as generic exotic decorations. Research meaning, ask relevant speakers/participants, respect permissions and represent contemporary culture beyond stereotypes. Locality can inform form, typography, language, story, photography and interaction without being visually literal.

## When to stop and revise
A user says “looks AI-generated” — investigate whether the problem is sameness, weak hierarchy, fake content, excessive chrome, inconsistent spacing, lack of recognizable visual assets or wrong creative thesis. Do not answer by adding more glass, gradients and animation. Conduct a structured critique and revise based on observed problems.

## Design-delivery report
For a completed substantial design, report briefly: product/audience, creative territory and why, sources consulted, what was implemented, screenshots/tests actually performed, files/commit locations and remaining limitations. Never equate “compiled” with “visually validated,” or “designed” with “deployed.”
