# 11 — Websites, Editorial Design, Content Trust and Conversion

## A website exists to support user decisions
A public business website's job is not merely to look impressive. It must help a visitor understand whether the offer fits, believe credible evidence, find relevant details, and take a next step. Editorial and service sites need distinctive narratives; transactional sites need clear procedures. Avoid forcing every brief into hero → three services → testimonials → CTA.

## Content architecture before surface
Write an inventory of truthful available content: name, services, geographical area, hours, prices/quote method, certifications, project proof, testimonials with consent, contact methods, booking/application forms and FAQs. If content is missing, mark it visibly as pending in staging; do not invent people, testimonials, locations or legal claims for production.

Map page purpose. A construction firm may need services, process, portfolio, location coverage, trust documents and quote request. An artist page may need releases, live booking, story, media kit and socials. A student accommodation platform may need search, listing detail, location, costs, application, viewing and eligibility. Each has different user journeys and SEO information architecture.

## Homepage composition
The first viewport should explain:
- identity and category in readable terms;
- relevant user value (not a vague ambition);
- primary call to action and, if justified, a secondary route;
- credibility that does not require misleading invented proof.
An enormous art-directed hero is acceptable if it helps the proposition and maintains usable navigation. But a user seeking a phone number should not need to scroll through five visual performances.

## Editorial hierarchy and storytelling
Use deliberate visual rhythm and authentic photography rather than grid fatigue. Allow some sections to be structured as editorial spreads, project detail narratives, photo captions, sequential proof, quotations, process steps or comparison tables. Distinctiveness can emerge from a local material palette, typographic rhythm, image cropping and verbal identity without compromising task completion.

## Conversion is not manipulation
Evidence-based conversion design emphasizes comprehension, reduced friction, clear next steps, truthful benefits and appropriate reassurance. Do not create dark patterns such as fake scarcity, misleading consent, false “only 2 left” banners or hidden price/fees. A conversion metric cannot excuse misleading design. Test leads for quality and user understanding, not only raw clicks.

## Calls to action
Use verbs that accurately represent the next step: “Request a quotation,” “View available rooms,” “Listen to the track,” “Book a performance” or “Send an enquiry.” Do not say “Get started” if the next screen is unexpectedly a payment form. Explain any key requirement before click when useful. Avoid saturating every section with identical floating buttons.

## Forms and contact
Keep labels visible, required fields clear, validation specific, keyboard focus evident and success messages actionable. Test real submission end-to-end (frontend validation, server/storage, email deliverability, confirmations), not just the clickable button. Preserve user data after recoverable errors; protect sensitive records with appropriate permissions and privacy. Public demo sites should not display a fake success message that creates the impression a customer enquiry was delivered.

## Information scent and navigation
Use understandable labels and paths. Visitors should be able to find services, proof, pricing, location and contact without interpreting clever category names. Descriptive headings and links help humans and search engines. Avoid duplicate paths from repeated card collections. Ensure mobile navigation doesn't obscure forms and important information.

## SEO as content architecture
Use meaningful title/meta descriptions, canonical URLs when needed, semantic heading structure, internal linking, useful differentiated page content, image alt descriptions, sensible performance, structured data only when accurate and correct indexing policies. Thousands of near-duplicate keyword pages harm quality. Real service area or property pages must contain genuine unique information, not generated location stuffing.

### Technical performance
Test:
- LCP, CLS and INP using credible measurement tools where possible;
- modern images, compression and meaningful loading priorities;
- reduced unnecessary JavaScript, animation and font overhead;
- low bandwidth and low-powered phones;
- link and navigation integrity across deployment base paths;
- asset caching and stale content after deploy;
- accessibility including keyboard, text zoom and contrast;
- form/API backend behavior and failure states.
No universal score guarantees sales or search ranking; thresholds are diagnostic.

## Brand expression online
Identity needs coordinated typography, photographic tone, icon system, grid, microcopy, colour roles and signature layout elements. A cheap website can be highly distinctive using good type, original photos and content-first architecture. A dark gradient plus many generic stock cards is not inherently premium.

## Content credibility checklist
- Legal name and public trading name clear where relevant.
- Contact address/phone/email verified and published with permission.
- Service coverage factual.
- Testimonials attributable and authorized; no fabricated endorsements.
- Dates, pricing, promotions and stock reflect current reality.
- Team/staff identity consensual and accurate.
- Portfolio work owned or authorized.
- Qualification and compliance badges accurate.
- Privacy/cookie notices aligned with actual collection.
- Copyright and image licences documented.

## Case—South African small service business
For a fixed-price first website offer, clarity may outperform elaborate motion. Show precisely what the price covers, what is excluded, what is needed from the client, actual delivery process, portfolio examples and contact. Account for WhatsApp inquiry flows, mobile-first use, lower bandwidth, affordable hosting and ongoing maintenance. Do not assume all clients want a high-cost enterprise system.

## Case—creator/artist
A creator brand needs authentic editorial identity and good media usability: clear artist name, track covers/links, short/long bios, performance clips, event history, booking inquiries and a kit for press. Protect subject identity; avoid invented chart performance or images unrelated to the actual artist. The visual system should support both fast social thumbnails and larger editorial pages.

## Case—ministry/nonprofit
Prioritize accessible service times, accurate location, leadership content, events, contact and giving transparency. Don't bury pastoral or community information inside ornamental animations. Treat donor and applicant data carefully; design respectful, consent-aware forms.

## Review scenario matrix
First-time desktop, first-time low-bandwidth mobile, keyboard-only user, screen-reader navigation, incorrect form entry, no image available, sold-out/closed application, long content, old cached deployment, broken data connection and a user looking only for an address.
